# Retrospective — Assessment

Check whether the retrospective faithfully reflects the requested period and helps the people involved learn. This is a conformance review of the reflection, not a new deliverable-quality gate.

## Review criteria

- **Scope and outcome:** The chosen scope may be one current or unfinished coding session as well as a milestone. The window, expected outcome, actual result, and incomplete or unobserved work are distinguished. The retro is not treated as proof that a product shipped or met its requirements.
- **Balanced reflection:** Successful practices receive real attention alongside difficulties. Neither category is fabricated to fill a template; a fully successful milestone may need no process change.
- **Independent source reconstruction:** The retrospective examines original accessible sessions and relevant child sessions, not just General's handoff. It inspects user interaction, delegation prompts, tools, loaded skills, retries, verification and handoffs where recorded. Source IDs make episodes traceable; compaction gaps and inaccessible sources stay visible.
- **Evidence:** Material observations identify session/message/tool-call IDs, decisions, commits, test results, participant feedback, or other sources. Distinguish direct observations, reported experience, hypotheses and tested causes. Missing systems/data are honestly reported.
- **Human perspective:** Participants can correct the provisional account, and disagreement or absent feedback is visible rather than replaced with agent-generated sentiment.
- **Continuity:** Earlier accepted experiments are checked for implementation and effect when evidence permits. Incomparable measurements are not presented as a trend.
- **Learning:** Lessons include useful context and limits, recognize repeatable success as well as failure, and do not promote correlations or a single incident into general rules.
- **Engineering-environment lens:** Navigation, information access, checks, instructions, and tool economy are investigated only where episodes warrant it. Mechanical-failure proposals inspect existing checks and execution paths before proposing new rules or tooling; a high tool count or missing CI alone is not an automatic finding.
- **Improvement decisions:** Any proposed experiment has a defensible purpose, manageable risk, and an observable follow-up. No proposal is required, and no change is silently applied.
- **Storage and boundaries:** No raw private transcripts, dispatch prompts, tool outputs or secrets are copied into durable learning. Access respects the host's existing permissions. There is one deep evidence-led retrospective method, with incomplete findings when history is inaccessible; no extra runtime dependencies, permissions or ceremonies.

Record specific findings with evidence and remaining uncertainty. Using this file does not by itself constitute independent review.
