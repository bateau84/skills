# Teach Me — Assessment

Review whether the explanation helps this learner form an **accurate, usable mental model** of the concept they asked to learn.

1. **Learner fit:** Is the level inferred conservatively from actual user signals, or checked when uncertainty matters? Does the explanation respect requests to skip basics, use code, or go deeper?
2. **Topic-first clarity:** Is the user's named concept defined before its subtopics, and are unfamiliar prerequisites introduced where needed rather than hidden in jargon or a bulk glossary?
3. **Conceptual correctness:** Does the model explain what the concept is for and why it works? Are simplifications and analogy limits clear enough to prevent a materially false generalization?
4. **Evidence and examples:** Are examples technically accurate for the described environment and version? Are consequential release-specific facts checked or transparently uncertain? Is a counterexample included when it prevents a common misconception?
5. **Teaching rhythm:** In a paced session, is the response focused on one central idea with a meaningful learner checkpoint, instead of dumping unrelated topics? Does it adapt to explicit requests for more or less depth?
6. **Session boundaries:** Do pause, resume, topic changes, and end-of-lesson requests work only in the lesson's context? Is the response honest about what context it can retain and whether any external tool was used?
7. **Safe scope:** Does a request to *learn* remain explanation rather than unrequested repository edits, commands, external actions, or invented permissions?

**Evidence:** Inspect the actual explanation and the prior turns that establish learner knowledge and session mode. For version-specific claims, check the cited source if one is available. A transcript saying "I followed teach-me" is not evidence of learner understanding.

**Disposition:** Treat material technical misinformation, misleading analogy limits, fabricated facts/memory, or unintended actions as substantive findings. Different but accurate analogies or preferred wording are not defects by themselves.
