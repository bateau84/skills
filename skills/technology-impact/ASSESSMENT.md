# Technology Impact Assessment

## Assessment criteria

Check whether the work's purpose and material consequences are supported, not whether seven headings exist. Select this perspective from the actual change even when its producer omitted an impact assessment.

- **Depth and reuse:** consequences, not patch size, determined depth. Consequential changes considered all seven dimensions; routine work did not acquire a ritual. Prior analysis still matches the current scope and assumptions.
- **Benefit and burden:** evidence identifies the actual beneficiary and distinguishes observed value from a hypothesis. New maintenance, operating cost and human work were not merely transferred to an unexamined actor. Check the sponsor's account against affected users and operators where evidence is available.
- **Meaning:** compare old and proposed promises in affected contracts, interfaces, docs and tests. Words such as verified or complete must not hide an unauthorized weakening.
- **Control:** trace who can choose, spend, access, override, revoke or exit. Inspect provider incentives and dependence as well as nominal permissions. Claimed control needs a usable path, not just a setting name.
- **Alternative uses:** plausible repurposing and composition were considered without treating possibility as permission or inventing unrelated threat models.
- **Disposition and proof:** material concerns have a supported disposition. Accepted mitigations reach their actual owner, dependent work and observable check. A risk paragraph, proposed test or green unrelated suite is not mitigation evidence.

Report the consequence, evidence or missing proof, affected claim and correction owner in the existing review. Missing optional paperwork alone is not a defect. Block only under the host's existing rules for broken obligations, missing required evidence or credible material failures; do not create product requirements or risk acceptance through review.
